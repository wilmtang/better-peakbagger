Photo settings

Photo Topos lets you draw a route line and climbing symbols on your own photo, upload it, and drop the link into a Peakbagger trip report. Everything stays on this device until you choose to upload.

Two ways to add a photo

In the trip report editor, the image button offers both:

  • Upload a photo… — opens Photo Topos. Choose a picture from this device, mark the route on it, and upload it. This needs a free ImgBB key, set up once. Photos you have already uploaded are in the same place, under Library, ready to insert again.
  • Paste a direct image link — if the picture is already online somewhere that allows other sites to display it, no key is needed. What counts as a direct link.

Better Peakbagger never uploads anything on its own. A photo leaves this device only when you choose Upload.

Setting up an ImgBB key

Peakbagger does not host trip-report images, so the picture has to live somewhere public and be linked in. Better Peakbagger uses ImgBB, a free image host, and uploads with your account rather than through any server of its own. That means the uploads are yours, and they follow ImgBB's terms of service.

  1. Create a free account at imgbb.com.
  2. Go to api.imgbb.com and choose Get API key. It is a long string of letters and numbers.
  3. Paste it into Settings → Activity creation → Trip report photos, or into the box at the top of the Photo Topos page, and save.
  4. Your browser will ask permission for Better Peakbagger to reach api.imgbb.com. Allow it — without it, uploads cannot happen.

The key is stored on this device only. It is never synced between browsers, never included in a backup, and never sent anywhere except ImgBB. Removing it stops new uploads; photos you have already uploaded keep working.

Setting a key up on a second computer means pasting it there too. Keep a copy of the key somewhere you can find it, such as a password manager.

Drawing the topo

Pick a photo and mark it up. The image description is optional, but worth writing: it is the alt text screen readers announce, it is what shows when the image cannot load, and library search matches it alongside the title.

  • Route draws the line. Click each point along the route; double-click, right-click, press Enter, or choose Done drawing to finish. The first point appears as soon as you click it, and a dashed line previews the next one.
  • Symbols stay selected after you place one, so a pitch full of bolts is a run of clicks. Press Esc or V when you are done placing.
  • Select moves anything. Drag a route's round handles to reshape it, and use the arrow keys to nudge by one pixel, or ten with Shift.

The panel on the right sets colour, opacity, and size. Pick a tool and it shows the style the next mark will take, so you can choose before you click; select a placed mark and it edits that mark instead, adding its rotation and anything else particular to it. Either way the next mark you draw inherits whatever you chose last. Opacity matters more than it sounds: a solid line drawn over the crux hides exactly the detail the reader came for. Dropping a mark to 50–70% keeps the rock visible underneath.

Routes also take a width, a dashed or dotted line — useful for the part of a route hidden behind an arête — an arrowhead, and a smooth curve.

Work saves to this browser as you go. Ctrl/Cmd+Z undoes, and Shift with it redoes.

What the symbols mean

There is no single legend every guidebook agrees on — the UIAA publishes a recommended set and publishers extend it — so these follow the shapes climbers read without a key. Each tool button shows the same symbol it draws.

Bolt
One fixed point of protection. A circle is the most widely read mark for a bolt.
Anchor
A fixed anchor or lower-off: two bolts slung to a master point.
Piton
A fixed peg — a bladed pin with an eye. Worth marking when its condition is part of the beta.
Rappel
A rappel or abseil station: a ring with the rope running down out of it.
Belay
The stance where a pitch ends, drawn as the bar the leader stops on.

Pitch adds a numbered label, and Text adds free wording — a grade, a warning, a feature name. Both can sit on a dark plate so they stay readable over pale rock or snow.

Uploading and inserting

Upload and insert flattens the photo and your marks into one new image and sends that to ImgBB. The uploaded file carries the picture only: no camera metadata, no location, no original file name, and nothing about the report it came from.

If you opened Photo Topos from a trip report, the public link is inserted into that report for you. If the report tab has gone away in the meantime, the upload still succeeds — find the photo under Library and use Copy URL.

An uploaded photo is never edited in place. Changing it makes a new version and a new link, so a report that already points at the old image keeps working.

The library on this device

The Library tab is this browser's own record of your topos. It is not an ImgBB gallery — ImgBB has no way to list your uploads back — and it is not shared between computers unless you back it up. Each card shows whether a photo is still a local draft or has been uploaded, and whether it has been used in a report.

  • Insert or Copy URL reuses an uploaded photo.
  • Edit as new version starts a fresh copy to change, leaving the published one alone.
  • Download project saves a .bpb-photo file holding the original picture and your annotations. This is the only copy of the original pixels outside this browser, so it is worth keeping for photos you care about. Note that the original file's own metadata travels with it.
  • Import project… reads one of those files back — on a new computer, or after clearing browser data. If the photo is already here, it comes in as a separate draft rather than replacing anything.

Clearing your browser's site data for this extension erases the library, including original images. The public ImgBB links keep working; the editable copies do not come back on their own.

Backing up to GitHub

If you have connected a GitHub repository under Settings → Backup & sync, the photo library can keep a record of itself there, in a file called photo-library.json. Turn it on under Photo library backup, where Back up photos, Restore from backup, and an automatic option live. The library page also has Back up now.

What it stores:

  • titles, image descriptions, and which reports used each photo;
  • the annotations — every route line, symbol, and label;
  • the public ImgBB links, image sizes, and checksums;
  • a note of anything you have removed, so a restore does not bring it back.

What it deliberately does not store:

  • the images themselves — GitHub holds the record, not the picture;
  • your ImgBB API key;
  • the links that delete a photo from ImgBB.

So a restore on a new computer gives you back the catalogue and the public links, and your annotations become editable again as soon as the matching original is on that device — which is what Import project… is for. Restoring always shows you the counts first and asks before it replaces anything.

Removing a photo

Remove… on a library card is a local action. It moves the photo to Recently Deleted, where it can be restored for 30 days before the local original and annotations are cleared. It does not touch the image on ImgBB, and it does not touch a trip report you have already saved — a report that links to that image still shows it.

That is deliberate. Beta photos are what other climbers came to your report for, and an image deleted from the host leaves a broken picture in every report that ever linked to it, including other people's. Better Peakbagger has no button that does that, and the link needed to do it is never put in a backup or an exported project.

If you really do need to delete the image from ImgBB

Check first that no report still uses it — the library card says whether the photo has been inserted anywhere.

The straightforward route is to sign in at imgbb.com and delete it from your account there.

ImgBB also returns a one-off deletion link at upload time, which this extension keeps in this browser's storage and nowhere else. Reaching it means opening your browser's developer tools on the Photo Topos page, going to the Application or Storage panel, opening the betterPeakbaggerPhotos database, and reading the deleteUrl in the secrets store for that photo's id. It is meant to take deliberate effort.

Using an image link instead

You can skip Photo Topos entirely and paste a link to a picture that is already online. Two things have to be true, and this is where most links fail:

  • The link must point at the image file itself, not at a page showing it. A direct link usually ends in .jpg, .jpeg, .png, .gif, or .webp. On most hosts you get one by right-clicking the picture and choosing Copy image address.
  • The host must allow other sites to display it. Many popular services do not. Google Photos, Google Drive, iCloud Photos, Dropbox, and OneDrive share links all point at a viewer page and block embedding, so they show as a broken image in a trip report even though the link works when you open it yourself. Facebook and Instagram links expire.

Hosts that do work include ImgBB, Peakbagger's own photo pages, Imgur, Flickr's direct image links, and any web space of your own. The link must start with https://.

When something goes wrong

The upload finished but the report did not get the link.
The photo is safe. Open Library and use Insert or Copy URL. This usually means the report tab was closed or reloaded while the upload was running.
A photo says the upload outcome is unknown.
The connection broke at a point where ImgBB may or may not have accepted the image. Better Peakbagger does not retry on its own, because that could publish the same picture twice. Check your ImgBB account, then either upload again or remove the entry.
A photo says the image is unreachable.
The public copy did not load when the library last looked. It may be a temporary ImgBB problem, or the image may have been deleted there. Your local record and annotations are untouched.
Uploads say permission is missing.
The browser has not been allowed to reach api.imgbb.com. Save the key again from Settings and accept the prompt.
The edited image is too large to upload.
ImgBB sets the size limit, and the message says what it is for your account. Start from a smaller photo — for a trip report, something around 2000 pixels wide is plenty.
The library is empty on my other computer.
Expected: the library lives in one browser profile. GitHub backup brings the records across, and Import project… brings the originals.
